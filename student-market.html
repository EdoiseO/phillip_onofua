I led the development of a marketplace where Toronto students can discover campus-relevant items, manage listings, negotiate in listing-linked conversations, and rely on a structured moderation system when something goes wrong.

The product problem

A marketplace only works when discovery, trust, and communication work together.

Students need more than a grid of products. They need to know who they are dealing with, whether a meetup location is practical, which listing a conversation belongs to, and what happens when content is misleading or unsafe.

I treated those concerns as one product system rather than separate pages. A listing remains connected to its seller, campus, images, favourites, conversation, reports, moderation history, and eventual lifecycle state.

How the product works

One listing moves through a complete, accountable workflow.

  1. 01Discover

    Search and category views surface active listings with price, campus, condition, and seller context.

  2. 02Evaluate

    The listing page combines media, description, seller identity, meetup location, and a direct message entry point.

  3. 03Coordinate

    A conversation remains anchored to the item while supporting text, reactions, photos, videos, and lifecycle-aware controls.

  4. 04Moderate

    Reports preserve evidence and route trusted staff to bounded review queues, private context, and explicit decisions.

01 · Discovery

Make the next useful item easy to recognize.

The desktop marketplace keeps navigation, search, categories, price, campus, condition, and negotiability visible without crowding the listings themselves.

  • Active listings are separated from drafts, pending review, rejected, sold, and retired records.
  • Search and category pages use bounded result windows instead of loading the entire catalogue.
  • Saved items and seller tools remain accessible without interrupting browsing.

02 · Listing lifecycle

Connect a seller’s draft to a buyer’s decision.

Sellers can build a draft, upload ordered images, submit it for review, respond to requested changes, and update an approved listing without silently losing work.

For buyers, the detail page turns that structured record into a clear decision: what the item is, how much it costs, who is selling it, where a meetup could happen, and how to start a conversation.

03 · Messaging

Keep the negotiation attached to the item.

Every buyer–seller conversation carries the listing card and participant identity into the thread. The composer supports text, emoji, photo and video attachments, while read state and reactions make active threads easier to scan.

When a listing or conversation closes, history and media remain readable while new writes are disabled. This preserves the agreement without pretending the transaction is still active.

04 · Moderation

Turn a report into an evidence-backed decision.

The admin area separates ordinary marketplace activity from trusted operations. Admin, moderator, and staff roles receive different capabilities, and every registry is filtered and bounded before a reviewer opens a record.

  • Reports can target a listing, profile, or specific conversation message.
  • The detail view combines the flagged evidence, full context, related reports, listing or participant information, and private moderator notes.
  • Decision details are explicit and private moderation text stays out of user notifications.

05 · Responsive product

Recompose the workflow for a phone, not just a narrower screen.

Mobile navigation prioritizes browse, sell, messages, and account actions. Cards become touch targets, sidebars become focused routes, and the conversation preserves the listing and composer without shrinking a desktop split view.

The mobile examples below show the complete path from discovering an item to negotiating in the message thread and checking the person behind the listing.

06 · Engineering depth

Make the difficult states as deliberate as the happy path.

The interface is only one layer of the work. The project also handles ambiguous network responses, concurrent edits, role changes, rejected content, closed conversations, private evidence, and uploaded media that must not become orphaned.

Identity and access

Derive trust from current account state.

Toronto school identity, moderation roles, and application standing are rechecked by trusted server and database paths instead of relying on editable browser state.

Data integrity

Make important writes replay-safe.

Stable operation identifiers, canonical payloads, row locks, revisions, and immutable records protect listing, messaging, report, and enforcement changes from accidental duplication.

Private moderation

Separate internal reasoning from public outcomes.

Private notes and decision records remain behind role checks, while notifications receive only the bounded, user-safe explanation intended for the affected account.

Media lifecycle

Bind uploads before they become product data.

Reserved upload paths, ownership checks, bounded attachment rules, and cleanup workflows keep listing and message media connected to the correct user and record.

What this project demonstrates

I can carry a product from visible interaction design into the database rules that keep it trustworthy.

Student Market of Toronto brings together responsive product design, authentication, relational data, secure media handling, real-time communication, failure recovery, and role-based moderation. The result is a portfolio project that behaves like a connected marketplace rather than a collection of isolated screens.
